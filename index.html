Heart rate notes

“I am fit if my HR is in the low-mid end of my target zone with moderate to high intensity exercise.”

True/False, “We want to target an INCREASED resting heart rate”: False (we want a lower resting heart rate).

Two ways to increase intensity on cardio machines: increase the speed and increase the incline/resistance.

CDC recommendation: children and adolescents should get 60 minutes of physical activity daily, with a minimum of 20 minutes of cardiovascular activity.

Muscular Fitness

Strength vs. endurance

Muscular strengthThe maximum force that can be generated by a specific muscle or muscle group
Muscular enduranceThe ability of your muscles to perform physical tasks and sustain repeated contractions over a long period of time

Training protocols

% of 1 Rep Max80%60%

Move-in day example

1 Rep Max calculator

Class example: 6 reps at 50 lbs = estimated 1RM of 60 lbs.

Terms

Repetition (rep)How many times you repeat a muscle movement
SetA group of consecutive repetitions (3 × 10 = 3 sets of 10 reps)
Super setsExercising opposite muscle groups back to back (e.g. pecs/lats)
One Rep Max (1RM)The maximum amount of weight one can lift in a single repetition for a given exercise. It’s used to set your load as a percentage of your 1RM: 60% for endurance, 80% for strength.

Law of Use

“That which is used develops… and that which is not used wastes away!”

(If you don’t use it, you lose it.)

Rest and recovery

A muscle group needs about 48 hours of rest between strength workouts.

Proper technique

  • Warm-up
  • Full range of motion (ROM)
  • Proper amount of weight
  • Controlled smooth movements
  • Use a spotter (free weights)
  • Adjust the machine

Why proper technique is important (2 reasons): to get the desired results, and to avoid injury.

Warm-up

Importance of a proper warm-up

  • Faster muscle contraction: muscles contract faster and more forcefully
  • Ligaments and tendons are more pliable and less prone to injury
  • Less cardiovascular stress
  • Accelerated nerve impulses
  • Psychological readiness

Opposing muscles

Work opposite muscles to avoid a muscle imbalance (quadriceps & hamstrings, pectorals & lats).

Training principles

Overload principle

The body will respond by getting stronger when increased demands are placed upon it. It must be gradual and progressive, because too much overload leads to injury and fatigue.

Principle of progression

Gradual increase of the demands placed on the body to get increased results. Start out slowly with lighter weights, then increase systematically as the body adapts.

Example: Milo of Croton carried a newborn calf every day until it grew into a bull.

Principle of specificity

The body adapts “specifically” to the demands placed upon it (force, speed, angle).

Benefits

Benefits of muscular fitness

  • Increased energy
  • Increased self-esteem
  • Improved physical performance
  • Injury prevention
  • Better sleep
  • Improved health
  • Lower levels of fat
  • Increased life expectancy
  • Less muscular fatigue
  • Athletic performance
  • Protects bone density

Body Composition

What it is

Body composition: the relative comparison of body fat to lean body mass (muscle, bone, organs). It compares both body fat and lean body mass.

Example: 150 lb body weight × 20% body fat

30 lbs120 lbs

Fat weightFat-free weight

Why know it: evaluate your health, plan a better program for weight management, evaluate your progress on an exercise and nutrition plan.

Functions of body fat

  1. Normal physiological functioning
  2. Protection of organs from bodily harm
  3. Insulation and body temperature regulation
  4. Absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E & K)
  5. Energy source (stored energy)

Two types of fat

Subcutaneous

Fat stored just underneath the skin; the fat you can feel on your arms and legs. It’s for warmth and protection, as well as storage of extra energy.

Visceral

Fat stored in and around internal organs and the torso, also known as intra-abdominal fat or organ fat. Excess can lead to increased risk for health problems (heart disease, type 2 diabetes). It’s usually the first fat to be lost in weight loss.

Fat vs. muscle

Two different tissues

MuscleFat
PropertiesFibrous, contractible and connective tissueLipids, adipose tissue
Weight / densityHeavier / more denseLighter / less dense
FunctionMoves the body and maintains postureProtection, insulation and a source of energy
Response to exerciseIncreases with exerciseDecreases with exercise

Fat can NOT be converted to muscle.

BMI

BMI (Body Mass Index): a measure of relative size based on the mass (weight) and height of an individual.

Still widely used to screen for weight categories that may lead to health problems.

Problem: BMI does not account for age, sex, ethnicity or muscle mass.

Fat cell timeline

    Swipe the timeline sideways to see all 4 stages.

    Fat cell notes

    • Fat cell development is rapid in the first few years of life.
    • During growth, fat cells increase in number.
    • Fat cell number is set by your early twenties. After this, they increase in size.
    • When energy intake exceeds expenditure, fat cells increase in size.
    • With fat loss, the size of fat cells shrinks, but not the number.
    • True/False, “The number of fat cells reduces in your lifetime”: False.
    • If fat cells have enlarged and energy intake keeps exceeding expenditure, fat cells can increase in number again.
    • Your lifestyle and dietary habits as a teenager are factors in determining your health as an adult.
